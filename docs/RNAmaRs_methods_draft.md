# RNAmaRs Methods — Nature Methods Draft

## Methods

### Overview

Alternative splicing is regulated by RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) that recognize short sequence motifs at specific positions relative to alternatively spliced exons. When differential splicing is observed between two phenotypic conditions (e.g., tumor versus normal tissue, drug-treated versus untreated cells), a key challenge is to identify which RBP(s) are responsible for the observed splicing changes. We present RNAmaRs (MRM-RBP Association Score), a computational framework that addresses this problem by integrating positional motif enrichment analysis with genome-wide RBP binding data from eCLIP experiments. RNAmaRs builds on the RNAmotifs algorithm (Cereda et al., Genome Biology, 2014), which identifies clusters of short RNA motifs (tetramers) enriched at specific positions around alternatively spliced exons and generates RNA splicing maps that reveal position-dependent regulatory signatures. RNAmaRs extends this approach by computing a composite association score between the positional motif enrichment profiles observed in a phenotypic splicing dataset and the known binding profiles of RBPs characterized by ENCODE eCLIP in HepG2 or K562 cell lines. The algorithm operates in two modes: a discovery mode that determines optimal analysis parameters for each reference RBP, and an application mode that ranks candidate RBPs by their association with the splicing changes observed in a new phenotypic comparison.

### RNAmotifs: motif discovery and RNA splicing maps

The RNAmotifs pipeline takes as input a set of alternatively spliced exons classified as enhanced (inclusion level increased relative to control; dIRank > 1), silenced (inclusion level decreased; dIRank < −1), or constitutive (unchanged; dIRank = 0). Each exon is specified by its genomic coordinates along with the coordinates of its flanking exons in a semicolon-delimited format. The pipeline can also accept rMATS Skipped Exon output directly, applying user-defined thresholds on inclusion level difference and false discovery rate to classify exons.

For each exon, four flanking regions are defined relative to the splice sites: region R1 (the upstream intron, spanning *d* bp upstream of the exon start, where *d* denotes the intron extent parameter, by default 300 bp), region R2 (the exon body extending *e* bp into the exon from the 5' splice site, where *e* denotes the exon extent parameter, by default 30 bp), region R3 (the downstream intron, spanning *d* bp downstream of the exon end), and region R4 (the distal flanking region). To prevent coordinate collisions when mapping positions from all four regions into a single positional vector, region centers are spaced at intervals of 2 × max(*e*, *d*) + 10 bp along a virtual coordinate axis.

The motif search scans each flanking region for 512 tetramers: 256 canonical tetramers composed of {A, C, G, T} and 256 degenerate tetramers using the IUPAC nucleotide ambiguity codes {R, Y, S, W, K, M, B, D, H, V}. The search is implemented in C++17 with OpenMP parallelization across all 512 motifs. For each tetramer, positions of occurrence are recorded relative to the splice sites, and clusters of occurrences are identified using a sliding window of half-width *hw* (default 15 bp). Clusters meeting a minimum height threshold *mh* (default 4 occurrences) and a percentage threshold *pth* (default 0.5) are retained.

Statistical significance of positional enrichment is assessed in two stages. First, at each position, a two-sided Fisher exact test compares the frequency of tetramer occurrences in enhanced (or silenced) exons versus constitutive control exons. The enrichment window parameter *ew* (default 30 bp) defines the width of the positional bin for counting. Second, empirical p-values are obtained by bootstrap resampling: the exon class labels (enhanced, silenced, constitutive) are permuted *N* times (default *N* = 10,000), and for each permutation, positional Fisher exact tests are recomputed. The empirical p-value at each position is the fraction of permutations yielding a Fisher p-value as extreme or more extreme than the observed value. Tetramers are considered significantly enriched if they pass both a Fisher p-value threshold (default 0.1) and an empirical p-value threshold (default 5 × 10⁻⁵) in at least one region for either enhanced or silenced exons. Multiple testing correction is applied via the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure within each region.

Significantly enriched tetramers are clustered by sequence and positional profile similarity. Sequence similarity is assessed by trimeric overlap: two tetramers share a trimer if at least half of their constituent trimers match, accounting for IUPAC degeneracy. Among sequence-similar tetramers, Pearson correlation of their positional enrichment profiles determines the ordering within each cluster. The combined Fisher score for each tetramer is computed as *S* = −2[log *p*_enh + log *p*_sil], aggregated across all positions, and tetramers are ranked by decreasing total score.

The output is an RNA splicing map: a matrix of positional enrichment scores across all four regions for each enriched tetramer, visualized as a multi-panel plot in which bar height encodes the combined Fisher score and colour encodes the relative contribution of enhanced (red) versus silenced (blue) exons. The enrichment vector for each tetramer, containing the per-position Fisher-based score, is retained for downstream association scoring.

### MRM-RBP association scoring

The association between a motif enrichment profile and an RBP binding profile is quantified by two complementary scores.

**SCORE1: Signal Recovery Rate.** This score quantifies how robustly the eCLIP binding signal for a given RBP is recovered when the eCLIP data are subsampled. During the discovery phase (described below), eCLIP peak data for each RBP are systematically downsampled, and the area under the curve (AUC) of signal recovery is precomputed for each RBP and splicing direction (enhanced or silenced exons). These AUC values, stored as a precomputed reference vector, reflect the intrinsic informativeness of each RBP's eCLIP data. For a given tetramer-RBP pair, SCORE1 is assigned as the AUC-based recovery metric for that RBP, ensuring that RBPs with more robust and reproducible eCLIP signal receive higher weight. SCORE1 is constant across tetramers for a given RBP and parameter combination, serving as a reliability weight.

**SCORE2: Profile Similarity.** This score measures the positional concordance between the motif enrichment profile from RNAmotifs and the eCLIP binding profile around the same set of exons. The eCLIP binding profile is constructed as follows. For each exon in the input set, a position matrix is built spanning all four regions (R1–R4), with each position extending from −*e* to +*d* bp relative to each splice site, where an additional half-window adjustment of *r* = 7 bp is applied to both the exon and intron extents to account for the sliding window used during motif clustering. eCLIP peak coordinates for the RBP of interest are intersected with this position matrix using a hash-set lookup: all genomic positions covered by eCLIP peaks are inserted into a per-chromosome hash set, and each position in the exon flanking regions is queried in O(1) time. For each position, a binary indicator records whether at least one eCLIP peak overlaps. This overlap computation is parallelized across exons using OpenMP. For minus-strand exons, the overlap vector is reversed to maintain a consistent 5'-to-3' orientation. To constrain memory usage, overlaps are computed for one RBP at a time: peaks are loaded, the overlap matrix is computed across all exons, scores are derived for every parameter combination that uses that RBP, and the overlap matrix is freed before proceeding to the next RBP. This sequential processing strategy keeps peak resident memory below 2 GB regardless of the number of reference RBPs.

The aggregate binding profile is then computed by one-sided Fisher exact test at each position, comparing the frequency of eCLIP overlap at enhanced (or silenced) exons harboring the tetramer of interest versus constitutive control exons. The resulting Fisher p-values are transformed to a binding score vector *b* = −2 log *p* at each position across the four regions. The eCLIP overlap depends only on the RBP identity and the intron extent parameter *d*, not on the clustering parameters *hw* or *ew*, and is therefore computed once per RBP and reused across the parameter grid.

The positional similarity between the motif enrichment vector *m* (from RNAmotifs) and the binding profile vector *b* (from eCLIP) is quantified by cosine similarity:

*SCORE2* = cos(*m*, *b*) = (*m* · *b*) / (||*m*|| × ||*b*||)

where the dot product and norms are computed over the adjusted positions that correspond to the original RNAmotifs positional coordinate system. SCORE2 ranges from −1 to 1, with higher values indicating greater concordance between where the motif is enriched and where the RBP binds.

The final MRM-RBP association score for each tetramer-RBP pair is the product:

*Association Score* = *SCORE1* × *SCORE2*

This multiplicative formulation ensures that a high association score requires both a reliable eCLIP signal (SCORE1) and strong positional agreement between motif enrichment and RBP binding (SCORE2). Association scores are computed separately for enhanced and silenced exons.

### Training exon preparation

The discovery mode requires, for each reference RBP, a set of differentially spliced exons identified from shRNA-mediated knockdown experiments in the same cell line. These training exon sets are prepared from ENCODE rMATS output as follows. Skipped exon events are extracted from SE.MATS.JunctionCountOnly.txt files. Cassette exons are filtered against the UCSC hg19 knownAlt table to retain only annotated cassette exon events. Exons with |Δmean(PSI)| > 0.1 and FDR < 0.1 are classified as alternatively spliced; exons with |Δmean(PSI)| ≤ 0.01 are classified as constitutive controls. Following the ENCODE convention where IncLevelDifference = KD − Control, exons with negative ΔΨ (inclusion decreased upon knockdown) are classified as enhanced (the RBP promotes inclusion), and exons with positive ΔΨ are classified as silenced (the RBP represses inclusion). The dIRank score is set to ±(1 + |ΔΨ| × 10) accordingly. Constitutive exons are randomly subsampled to a maximum of 5,000. Optionally, exons are further filtered to those with eCLIP peak overlap within 300 bp (intron) or 30 bp (exon) of splice sites, with adaptive windows for short introns (< 600 bp) or short exons (< 60 bp). The output for each RBP is a semicolon-delimited file in RNAmotifs input format.

### Parameter optimization (discovery mode)

The discovery mode determines the optimal RNAmotifs parameters for each RBP in a reference panel. The reference panel comprises 15 RBPs for HepG2 (HNRNPC, HNRNPK, HNRNPU, NCBP2, PRPF8, PTBP1, QKI, RBFOX2, RBM22, SF3A3, SF3B4, SRSF1, U2AF1, U2AF2, UCHL5) and 13 RBPs for K562 (AGGF1, EFTUD2, FXR1, HNRNPU, PRPF8, PTBP1, PUS1, RBM15, SF3B4, SRSF1, TARDBP, U2AF1, U2AF2).

RBPs are processed sequentially to limit resource consumption. For each RBP, RNAmotifs is executed across the parameter grid defined by the half-window *hw* ∈ {5, 15, 25, 35} and the enrichment window *ew* ∈ {30, 50, 100, 200, 300}, yielding 20 parameter combinations per RBP (the grid can optionally include intron extent *d* ∈ {300, 500}, doubling to 40 combinations). For each parameter combination, the pipeline: (1) runs RNAmotifs on the RBP's knockdown exon set with *N* = 1,000 bootstrap iterations; (2) performs tetramer selection and enrichment classification; and (3) computes the association score against the RBP's own eCLIP binding profile. The optimal parameter set for each RBP is selected as:

(*hw*\*, *ew*\*) = argmax_{*hw*, *ew*} mean(*SCORE1* × *SCORE2*)

where the mean is taken across all enriched tetramers.

Processing state is persisted in a JSON manifest after each parameter combination, enabling crash-safe resumption. Intermediate results for non-optimal parameter combinations are removed after the best is selected to conserve disk space. Non-optimal scoring directories are deleted while the optimal sweep directory is preserved for use in application mode.

The discovery phase also computes two reference datasets. First, a normalized eCLIP binding profile matrix (PEAK) is generated for each cell line via a dedicated invocation of the `rnamotifs_mars_score` binary with the `--compute-peak` flag. For each training RBP, the knockdown exon set is loaded alongside the RBP's eCLIP peaks, and eCLIP overlap is computed across all positions in the four flanking regions (R1–R3). At each position, a one-sided Fisher exact test compares eCLIP overlap frequency at enhanced (or silenced) exons versus constitutive controls, yielding a score of −2 log *p*. The maximum score within each region is recorded, producing a six-element vector per RBP (R1_enh, R2_enh, R3_enh, R1_sil, R2_sil, R3_sil). Each RBP's vector is normalized to the range [0, 1] by dividing by its maximum element. The resulting matrix is stored as a TSV file and used during heatmap visualization to annotate each RBP with its characteristic binding pattern. Second, an AUC reference file recording the best association score per RBP is generated for use as SCORE1 weights in application mode.

The output of the discovery phase is: (i) a CSV table mapping each RBP to its optimal parameter pair (RNAmotifs_optimal_parameters.csv), (ii) the normalized PEAK binding profile matrix, and (iii) the AUC-based signal recovery metrics. Together, these constitute the trained reference used in application mode.

### RBP identification from phenotypic splicing data (application mode)

In application mode, the researcher provides a set of differentially spliced exons from a phenotypic comparison where the responsible RBP is unknown. The pipeline proceeds in three phases.

**Phase 1: Multi-parameter RNAmotifs sweep.** The set of (*hw*, *ew*) parameter combinations required across all reference RBPs is determined from the optimal parameters table. Only the unique combinations are executed, avoiding redundant computation. The RNAmotifs pipeline is run for each required (*hw*, *ew*) combination using the user-provided exon set, generating enriched tetramers and positional enrichment profiles under each parametrization. Each completed run is converted to the internal format expected by the scoring module, including tetramer selection, enrichment classification by region, and generation of per-tetramer splicing map CSVs. The multi-parameter sweep is managed by an orchestration layer that tracks completion status via a JSON manifest, enabling crash-safe resumption and caching of completed runs.

**Phase 2: Association score computation.** For each RBP in the reference panel, the association score is computed using that RBP's optimal parameters. The computation is implemented in C++17 with OpenMP parallelization (`rnamotifs_mars_score`). To minimize memory usage, RBPs are processed sequentially: for each RBP, eCLIP peaks are loaded and the overlap matrix is computed across all input exons, then SCORE1 and SCORE2 are computed for every parameter combination that maps to that RBP, and finally the overlap matrix is released before proceeding to the next RBP. This design keeps peak memory usage below approximately 2 GB regardless of the number of reference RBPs, compared to the 15–20 GB that would be required if all RBP overlaps were cached simultaneously.

The enriched tetramers from each parameter combination are classified as enhanced- or silenced-associated based on their regional enrichment direction. For each tetramer–RBP pair, SCORE2 is computed by: (i) identifying exons containing the tetramer in enriched regions, (ii) constructing the positional eCLIP binding profile via Fisher exact test at each position, and (iii) computing the cosine similarity between the binding profile and the motif enrichment profile from the corresponding splicing map. SCORE1 is assigned from the precomputed AUC reference. The final association score is the product SCORE1 × SCORE2. Output files include per-RBP association score matrices (TSV format), per-parameter SCORE1 and SCORE2 diagnostic matrices (written to a `diagnostics/` subfolder within the scores output directory), per-parameter tetramer enrichment summary tables, and an RBP ranking table ordered by mean association score.

**Phase 3: Heatmap visualization.** The association scores are aggregated into a final RBP-by-tetramer matrix. RBPs are ranked by the mean association score across all enriched tetramers (computed separately for enhanced and silenced directions). The results are visualized as a heatmap in which rows represent ranked RBPs, columns represent enriched tetramers (ordered by decreasing combined enrichment score), and circle size and colour encode the association score (viridis palette). Row annotations include the mean association score (bar plot), a normalized binding profile matrix (PEAK) showing the peak eCLIP signal in each region (R1–R3) for enhanced and silenced exons, and optionally the log2 fold-change in RBP gene expression from a user-provided DESeq2 differential expression analysis. Column annotations display the per-region enrichment significance for each tetramer, read from the summary results tables. Alongside each row, the RNA splicing map from the corresponding optimal-parameter RNAmotifs run is rendered, enabling visual comparison of the positional enrichment pattern for each candidate RBP.

### Implementation and resource requirements

RNAmaRs is implemented as a Python 3 orchestrator (`rnamotifs-mars`) that coordinates C++17/OpenMP binaries for motif discovery and association scoring with R scripts for heatmap generation. The motif discovery components (tetramer search, positional mapping, counting, and bootstrap FDR) are written in C++17 with OpenMP parallelization and require no external C++ libraries. The association scoring module (`rnamotifs_mars_score`) is also implemented in C++17 with OpenMP, computing Fisher exact tests, eCLIP overlap, and cosine similarity natively. The heatmap visualization uses R (version ≥ 4.0) with `ComplexHeatmap` (Bioconductor) for heatmap rendering, and `data.table`, `dplyr`, and `stringr` for data manipulation. All inter-module data exchange uses tab-separated text files (TSV), eliminating dependencies on R-specific serialization formats.

The pipeline enforces resource limits of 10 CPU cores and 45 GB RAM maximum. A typical application-mode run with 15 RBPs, approximately 8 unique parameter combinations, 10,000 bootstrap iterations, and 4,000–5,000 input exons completes in approximately 2–4 hours on a 10-core workstation, depending on the number of enriched tetramers identified. The multi-parameter RNAmotifs sweep (Phase 1) dominates runtime, as each parameter combination requires an independent run including bootstrap FDR estimation. Phase 2 (association scoring) processes RBPs sequentially, keeping peak memory under 2 GB; its runtime is typically under 30 minutes for 15 RBPs. A full discovery-mode training run with 1,000 bootstrap iterations per parameter combination completes in approximately 9–12 hours for one cell line. Both modes support crash-safe resumption via JSON manifests that record per-task completion status.

Input data requirements include: (i) a semicolon-delimited exon file with genomic coordinates and splicing change scores (for application mode) or per-RBP knockdown exon files (for discovery mode), (ii) processed eCLIP peak files in BED format from ENCODE for the selected reference cell line, (iii) precomputed optimal parameters, AUC-based signal recovery metrics, and normalized binding profiles from the discovery-mode training phase (for application mode), and (iv) a reference genome assembly (hg19, hg38, mm9, or mm10). An optional DESeq2 differential expression table can be provided to annotate the heatmap with RBP expression changes in the phenotypic comparison, offering an orthogonal line of evidence for RBP involvement. All code is available under the GPL-2.0-or-later license.

### Extension to intron retention events

In addition to cassette exon (skipped exon) events, RNAmotifs supports intron retention (IR) analysis via the `--event-type RI` flag. For IR events, the four analysis regions are redefined to reflect the distinct regulatory architecture of retained introns:

- **R1**: upstream exon (the exonic region approaching the 5' splice site, extent controlled by `in_exon`)
- **R2**: first half of the retained intron (from the 5' splice site toward the intron midpoint, extent controlled by `in_intron`)
- **R3**: second half of the retained intron (from the intron midpoint toward the 3' splice site, extent controlled by `in_intron`)
- **R4**: downstream exon (the exonic region after the 3' splice site, extent controlled by `in_exon`)

This mapping places the core regulatory elements — branch point sequences, polypyrimidine tracts, and intronic splicing regulatory elements — within the central regions R2 and R3, where they can be detected by the positional tetramer enrichment analysis. The `in_exon` and `in_intron` parameters retain their biological semantics: `in_exon` controls the extent into exonic sequence and `in_intron` controls the extent into intronic sequence, regardless of event type. For short retained introns (shorter than 2 × `in_intron`), R2 and R3 automatically meet at the intron midpoint, providing full coverage of the retained intron.

IR events are encoded in the input file with an optional 10th field `;RI` appended to each line. The four coordinate fields represent the upstream exon start, the 5' splice site (upstream exon end), the 3' splice site (downstream exon start), and the downstream exon end. Training exon sets for IR are prepared from rMATS RI.MATS output using `prepare_mars_exons.py --event-type RI`, which classifies intron retention events by the same PSI and FDR thresholds used for skipped exons, but bypasses the UCSC cassetteExon annotation filter.

The RNA splicing map visualization for IR events uses the same lattice-based rendering as skipped exon maps, with the region layout adapted to reflect the IR geometry: flanking exonic regions (R1, R4) are rendered as narrow shaded panels, while the retained intron halves (R2, R3) form the wide central unshaded regions. Top-axis tick marks indicate the 5' and 3' splice site positions. The eCLIP overlap computation in the association scoring module similarly adapts its position scan to use the 5' and 3' splice sites as anchor points, scanning `in_exon` base pairs into the flanking exons and `in_intron` base pairs into the retained intron from each splice site.
